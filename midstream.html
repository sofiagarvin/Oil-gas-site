Midstream (Transport & Processing)

Midstream connects supply to demand. It gathers, processes, transports, stores, and exports oil, gas, and NGLs. Business models often emphasize contracted cash flows and high reliability.

Core functions
Field → market

What midstream does

  • Gathering: move production from pads to central facilities.
  • Processing: remove impurities; separate NGLs; condition gas to specs.
  • Transmission: long-haul pipelines and interconnects.
  • Storage: balance seasonality and market timing.
  • Terminals: blending, loading, custody transfer.
  • Export: connect to global markets via docks and LNG terminals.
Commercial
How money flows

Common revenue models

  • Fee-based: tariff per unit moved or stored.
  • Take-or-pay / MVC: shipper pays for reserved capacity or minimum volume.
  • Percent-of-proceeds: processor shares value of extracted liquids.
  • Commodity-exposed: spreads between purchase and sale prices (higher risk).
  • Blend: contract portfolio often mixes stability and upside.

Quick mental model
One sentence

Midstream = infrastructure + contracts

  • Physical networks deliver molecules; contracts allocate volume and price risk.
  • Bottlenecks create pricing differentials; new capacity competes them away over time.
  • Strong systems emphasize safety, uptime, and disciplined growth.
