Downstream (Refining & Products)

Downstream turns crude into usable products—gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, lubricants, and petrochemical feedstocks. Profitability depends on margins (spreads), utilization, complexity, and logistics.

Process
Crude → slate

What refineries do

  • Distillation: separates crude into fractions by boiling range.
  • Conversion: cracks heavy molecules into lighter fuels.
  • Treating: removes sulfur/impurities to meet specs.
  • Blending: mixes components to required grades (octane, vapor pressure).
  • Distribution: racks, pipelines, barges, trucks to end markets.
Economics
Where profit comes from

Key drivers of profitability

  • Crack spread: product prices minus crude input cost (proxy for margin).
  • Yield optimization: produce more of what is valuable given market demand.
  • Logistics: advantaged access to crude supply and product outlets.
  • Regulatory: specifications and environmental requirements affect costs and blends.
  • Maintenance timing: turnarounds and reliability planning are strategic.

Quick mental model
One sentence

Downstream = margin capture + logistics

  • Refining is a continuous process business that monetizes spreads, not volumes alone.
  • Best operators pair advantaged feedstock access with resilient distribution and high uptime.
  • Market structure (local supply/demand) can matter as much as technical configuration.
